01 / THE PROJECT

More than a finished robot.

Engineering is a cycle of ideas, prototypes, testing, and decisions. The final mechanism is only part of the story.

This portfolio documents contributions to a 2026 FIRST Robotics Competition robot rebuild across mechanical design, fabrication, programming, and team leadership. A central area of involvement was adapting and iterating on the robot's intake for a MAXSwerve drive base.

The intake began as the standard KitBot intake. It was retrofitted from the KitBot drive base to the MAXSwerve drive base to take advantage of swerve drive's competitive benefits, which required changes to its height, pitch, mounting hardware, and overall width.

02 / HOW THE WORK HAPPENS

From first sketch
to field-ready thinking.

FRC robot building is a cycle of strategy, design, fabrication, programming, testing, and refinement. Each stage informs the next until the robot can perform reliably under competition conditions.

01
UNDERSTAND THE PROBLEM

Define the challenge.

Start with what the robot needs to accomplish. Identify game-piece geometry, space limitations, available hardware, and the conditions the mechanism must handle. Translate these into testable requirements, such as reliable acquisition, controlled movement, protected game pieces, and smooth handoff to the next subsystem.

↗
02
DESIGN + FABRICATE

Turn concepts into hardware.

Explore mechanism layouts, check fit and accessibility, and fabricate parts for assembly. For this rebuild, the narrow KitBot intake had to be adapted to a wider square MAXSwerve base, with revised height, pitch, and mounting hardware. In an FRC build, these decisions also account for serviceability, structural strength, robot packaging, and fast repairs between matches.

↗
03
PROGRAM + INTEGRATE

Make the mechanism respond.

Develop and refine the intake's control behavior, integrate it with the rest of the robot, and check how commands translate into real-world motion. Sensor feedback, motor behavior, driver controls, and subsystem handoffs all have to work together so the mechanism is predictable and easy to operate.

↗
04
TEST + ITERATE

Learn from every run.

Observe what works and what does not, isolate the cause of a problem, make a targeted change, and test again. Foam was added to the intake to prevent balls from rolling out, then experimentally shaved until it stopped rollout consistently while still allowing balls to feed from the hopper into the shooter. This same loop applies throughout FRC: test individual functions first, then test complete scoring cycles, driver operation, and repeated match-like runs.

↗
03 / FEATURED SUBSYSTEM

The intake
iteration story.

A working mechanism takes more than a good idea. The intake connects physical design with software behavior, making it a useful case study in cross-disciplinary engineering.

The intake began as the standard KitBot intake and was retrofitted to the MAXSwerve drive base for the competitive advantage of swerve drive. That retrofit required adjusting the intake's height, pitch, and mounting hardware, while adapting its narrow geometry to the wider square base.

To improve reliability, foam was added to keep balls from rolling out of the intake. The foam was experimentally shaved until it consistently prevented rollout while still allowing balls to feed from the hopper into the shooter.
