01 / THE PROJECT

More than a kit.

Engineering is a cycle of ideas, prototypes, testing, and decisions. The process leads to a better understanding of the problem and a more effective solution.

Project description: The 2026 FRC robot rebuild focused on creating a reliable, competition-ready machine while adapting a standard KitBot intake to a MAXSwerve drive base.

This portfolio documents contributions to FRC team 8303's 2026 FIRST Robotics Competition robot rebuild across mechanical design, fabrication, programming, and team leadership. Your role: I helped define resources, develop the intake concept, gather feedback, build prototypes, test designs, and iterate toward a final mechanism.

The solution: The intake began as the standard KitBot intake and was retrofitted from the KitBot drive base to the MAXSwerve drive base. Its height, pitch, mounting hardware, and width were adjusted so the narrower mechanism could work on the wider square base.

02 / HOW THE WORK HAPPENS

From first sketch
to field-ready thinking.

FRC robot building is a cycle of strategy, design, fabrication, programming, testing, and refinement. Each stage informs the next until the robot can perform reliably under competition conditions.

01
DEFINE RESOURCES

Start with what is available.

Identify the game-piece requirements, available KitBot parts, MAXSwerve packaging, tools, materials, time, and team skills. These resources establish the boundaries for a practical FRC solution.

↗
02
INITIAL IDEA

Choose a direction.

Begin with the standard KitBot intake and identify how it can be adapted to the MAXSwerve base. Consider intake geometry, driver visibility, ball control, handoff to the hopper, and the competitive advantages of swerve drive.

↗
03
GATHER FEEDBACK

Improve the idea together.

Share the concept with teammates, drivers, and mentors. Feedback helps reveal packaging conflicts, usability concerns, likely failure points, and changes that will make the mechanism easier to build and operate.

↗
04
PLAN + IMPLEMENT

Turn feedback into action.

Plan the mounting changes, revised height and pitch, wider base fit, materials, and build order. Implement the planned changes while keeping the design serviceable and compatible with the rest of the robot.

↗
05
OFF-ROBOT PROTOTYPE

Build the essentials first.

Construct a prototype away from the robot using only the parts needed to prove the core function. Check roller spacing, alignment, game-piece control, and the hopper-to-shooter handoff before committing to a full installation.

↗
06
TEST + ITERATE

Learn from every run.

Test the bare prototype, gather feedback, and adjust the design until it satisfies the initial goal. For this intake, foam was added to prevent balls from rolling out, then shaved through testing until it retained balls while still feeding the shooter.

↗
07
ROBOT INTEGRATION

Test the real constraints.

Install the satisfying prototype on the robot, gather feedback on its implementation, and test it with the drive base, hopper, shooter, controls, and other subsystems. Continue gathering feedback and iterating on the design until the full system meets the goal.

↗
08
FINAL BUILD + PRACTICE

Manufacture, install, and prepare.

Once the planned changes are complete and the goal is satisfied, manufacture the final design with its intended materials. Install the final mechanism, perform a final test, and practice repeated robot cycles so the team is ready to use it in competition.

↗
03 / FEATURED SUBSYSTEM

The intake
iteration story.

A working mechanism takes more than a good idea. The intake connects physical design with software behavior, making it a useful case study in cross-disciplinary engineering.

Your role: I helped lead the intake's transition from concept to competition-ready mechanism by coordinating feedback, prototyping, testing, and design changes.

The solution: The intake began as the standard KitBot intake and was retrofitted to the MAXSwerve drive base for the competitive advantage of swerve drive. That retrofit required adjusting the intake's height, pitch, and mounting hardware, while adapting its narrow geometry to the wider square base.

To improve reliability, foam was added to keep balls from rolling out of the intake. The foam was experimentally shaved until it consistently prevented rollout while still allowing balls to feed from the hopper into the shooter.

05 / WHAT COMES NEXT

Every iteration
is information.

Impact: The strongest FRC robots come from teams that turn every test run into useful information, communicate changes clearly, and keep refining the machine until it is ready for the field. This process turned a standard KitBot intake into a mechanism adapted for the MAXSwerve robot, with reliable ball retention and feeding built into the final design.
